4. If watching anime taught Japanese, you'd already be fluent.

6. If free apps worked, why does everyone you know quit them?
7. If you've "learned" 900 words in an app, why can't you use ten of them?

   *(the sharpest one — it proves NOT-YOUR-FAULT with their own evidence)*

## G. SALES PAGE (wordstick.app) — full copy, diagnose → solve → prescribe
Ad→page congruency: page opens by RE-OPENING the ad's problem in the same dialect.
Motivational, never educational, until the "how" section. Demo video sits above the fold.

### 1. Headline block
**You did the streak. You tapped the games on the bus.**
**And when it mattered, nothing came out.**
Sub: How busy people go from "I quit Duolingo twice" to real Japanese words that stick —
out loud, hands-free, in the dead minutes they already have.
[▶ demo video]  [Start free — 7 days]

### 2. The diagnosis (truth proves itself — their exact experience, no questions asked AT them)
You've put in real hours. Maybe hundreds. You can *recognize* plenty of Japanese —
when it's written in front of you, with four buttons to choose from.
Then someone actually speaks to you — and you go blank.
That gap has a name, and it isn't "bad at languages."

### 3. The why (not-your-fault + the mechanism in plain words — the aha is the sale)
Apps like the one you quit train **recognition**: the answer is on the screen,
and you pick it. Speaking is the opposite skill — pulling a word out of nothing,
with no buttons to lean on. You practiced picking answers, thousands of times,
and got genuinely good at it. Nobody ever made you practice *producing* them.
You didn't fail the method. The method never trained the thing you actually wanted.

### 4. Invalidate the alternatives (obstacles, one line each)
More streaks train more recognizing. Flashcards you flip are recognizing with extra steps.
Anime is listening to people who never wait for your answer. Textbooks are reading about
swimming. None of them put a word in your mouth.

### 5. What to do next (2.0: tell them WHAT, the app is the prescription)
Train recall out loud, in the minutes you already lose every day.
**WordStick says the meaning — you say the Japanese — before the word ever appears.**
No hands, no screen, no typing. The app listens for you to finish, then moves on,
like a patient friend who never judges a wrong answer. Words you produce out loud
come back when you need them. Words you only recognize don't. That's the whole trick.

### 6. What it feels like (desire stack, with transitions)
The first week it's a game you play against the windshield.
Then one morning a word comes out before you think of it — and that's the moment
it stops being studying. Because this was never about words. It's about the day
someone speaks Japanese to you and something *comes back out* — and the version
of you who "always wanted to learn" finally did.

### 7. KEEP block
Nothing gets added to your life. No study hour, no desk, no schedule.
Your commute stays your commute — it just quietly turns into the lesson.

### 8. What you DON'T have to do (removals)
No typing. No flashcards to shuffle. No screen-staring. No streak guilt.
No judgment — no one hears your accent but the app, and it doesn't care.

### 9. How it works (the 10% — three steps, no jargon)
1. Press play when you sit down in the car (or bus, or on the walk).
2. Say each word out loud when it asks. It hears you and keeps pace.
3. The app remembers what you struggled with and brings it back at the right time,
   spacing words further apart as they get stronger.
That's it. About ten minutes a session.

### 10. Price as filter + risk reversal
Free for 7 days — the real thing, not a demo. Then $8.99/month or $59.99/year.
Cancel in two taps. If a week of commutes doesn't put more Japanese in your mouth
than your last three months of streaks, quit and pay nothing.

### 11. Remove the retreat (gentle version — time, not shame)
The streak felt safe because it was *something*. But "something" has cost you —
how many months so far? — and it will happily take the next ones too.
The dead minutes are getting spent either way. The only question is on what.

### 12. Final CTA
**Start free. Tomorrow's drive to work is lesson one.**
[Start my 7 days]
