# Installing Compliance, applied to WordStick

Source: NHB Pro Theory Call, 30 Apr 2026, "Installing Compliance" (2 h 45 m).
Digested 26 Sep 2026. Owner: "it is incredible, we must apply it."

## The call in one paragraph

Marketing is an argument. Overt arguments (claims, hard sells, "buy now") make
people push back. The mastery is getting people to argue with themselves: plant
a small thing, let them do it, let the THING prove itself, and the conclusion
becomes theirs. Compliance is built in small steps ("gradients"): every small
yes makes the next yes easier, every no attaches to your name and makes the next
ask harder. So never give them a chance to say no. The size of the JUMP causes
resistance, not the size of the ask. Behaviour is the only truth: what they do
tomorrow, not what they say today. Value is a removed obstacle in their hands
right now. The word "free" up front installs a free mindset that kills the back
end, so flip it: "try it for seven days and decide; you pay nothing for seven
days." Target spenders, not havers. The $5 lesson: partnerwithpaul.com took off
not from its copy but because customers made five real dollars immediately and
became believers. "The thing has to prove itself, not you."

## What WordStick already does right (keep)

- The drill is a yes-machine: every card is a tiny doable ask (say the word)
  with no trade-off. That is the compliance loop the call describes.
- The honest count ("you own N words, proven cold") is THEIR evidence, not ours.
- The morning check is the $5 moment: yesterday's words, still there, out of
  their own head. That is the believer event.
- Onboarding requires zero learning (login, name, sticker, round 1).
- No "free" in the first round's copy; the card ask is one screen, with a way back.

## What changes

### 1. Flip "free" to an afterthought (applied on staging, v9.60)

| Where | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Paywall title | Start your free week | Try the whole thing for 7 days |
| Paywall yearly button | Start free week / Free for 7 days, then $X a year | Try it for 7 days / Decide at the end of the week. Nothing to pay for 7 days, then $X a year |
| Paywall monthly button | Free for 7 days, then $X a month | Nothing to pay for 7 days, then $X a month |
| Paywall exit | Not now, keep the free plan | Keep going as I am for now |
| Trial nudge button | Start my free week | Try the whole thing for 7 days |
| Trial nudge exit | Not now | Keep going as I am |
| Landing CTAs | Start free, 7 days / Start my 7 days | Try it for 7 days |
| Landing meta + pricing | 7-day free trial / Free for 7 days | Try it for 7 days, decide after / Decide after 7 days |

Why the exit wording matters: "Not now" is a no, and every no attaches to
WordStick. "Keep going as I am" is a small yes that leaves the door open.

### 2. Let the thing prove itself before the card ask (owner decision)

Today the card screen shows right after round 1. The call says the believer
moment is the RESULT, and our result is the morning check: words from
yesterday, still there. Proposal: ask for the card the morning after, right
after the check, with the count on the screen ("You just proved 8 words cold
after a night's sleep. Try the whole thing for 7 days."). Round 1 ends with a
softer ask only: "Come back tomorrow for the check" (a yes nobody refuses).
This changes the 23 Sep card-after-round-1 decision, so it is the owner's call.
Cost: some people never come back on day 2. Gain: the ones who do have their
own proof, and the ask is a small step instead of a jump.

### 3. Measure behaviour, not opt-ins (for the $300 test)

Add one number to the test's scoreboard: came back the next day and did the
check. It is the true signal of value and predicts trial-to-paid better than
trial starts. Keep the three kill lines; add day-2 return as the diagnostic.

### 4. Ads and posts are the first compliance step (content plan)

Every ad and organic post gives one tiny thing to DO with no trade-off: "Say
ありがとう three times, out loud, right now. That word is yours." The reader
does it, it works, first yes banked before the click. No claims to argue with.
The ad copy bank (docs/ad-copy-bank-2026-09-06.md) needs a pass for this: swap
claim-led hooks for do-this-now hooks.

### 5. Target spenders (ads audience)

Not "people interested in Japanese". People who already PAY for Japanese:
class-goers (Minna no Nihongo, community classes), Duolingo Super and Busuu
subscribers, JLPT registrants, Japan-trip bookers. Spending behaviour, not
interest.

### 6. Gradient inside the app (small, later)

Price ladder is fine (yearly first is one price, not a ladder; leave it).
Where the gradient matters is asks: round of 10 before 30, one sticker before
the album, one word in Talk before a sentence. Talk's teacher instructions
already adapt down; add "never ask something the learner can fail cold; offer
the first sound first." (Talk is on the anchor, so this rides with Talk work.)

## Not applying

- "N people said yes in the last 24 hours" around the button: only with real
  numbers, never invented.
- "Money will come" spending installation: not our market's currency. Our
  currency is the count of words that stick; sell that.
