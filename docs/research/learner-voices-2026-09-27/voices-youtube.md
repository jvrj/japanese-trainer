# Voices from YouTube comments: Japanese learners (harvested 27 Sep 2026)

**Scope:** 54 videos, **11,925 comments pulled** (top-sorted, up to 400 per video, replies included) with yt-dlp. 9,799 of them were 30+ characters and got keyword-scanned; about 2,900 matched a theme and were read by hand, highest-liked first. Every quote below is a real comment, cut to under 15 words (an ellipsis marks a cut). The format is (likes · link).

**About the counts:** "n≈" is how many comments matched that theme's keywords across all 9,799. It's a rough guide, not an exact census. Keyword matching over-counts some themes (like "forget") and under-counts others (like commuting). Bias warning: the sample leans toward young, anime-driven self-studiers. The 35+ traveller/partner buyer is a real but smaller voice here.

## What this means for WordStick (one line each)

- **The biggest pain is "years in and still can't speak."** WordStick's out-loud-first drill answers that directly. Lead with it and not with "vocab".
- **Streaks are loved and hated in the same breath.** Keep the honest "words truly nailed" count as the headline. Never add hearts or paywall-for-mistakes, which is the most-liked complaint in the whole set (4,900 likes).
- **"Forgot everything by day 3" and "I feel stupid when I forget a word I knew" are real.** The next-morning check should frame forgetting as normal and useful, never as failure.
- **Commuters/drivers are a thin but specific slice on YouTube (about 22 comments),** mostly Pimsleur users. The hands-free angle is a niche wedge here, not the mass message. One driver said audio in the car cost them focus, so keep it listen-and-say, never screen.
- **Pimsleur is the closest loved rival** ("REMEMBER key conversations", "walking, running or in the car"). Its complaints are price ($550), no text, and running out of levels. WordStick can be "Pimsleur-style out loud, at app price, that never runs out".
- **Travellers with a date and heritage/partner learners carry the strongest stakes.** They want restaurants, stations, grandparents and a spouse's family, not anime. Target those circumstances in copy.
- **Older learners (50s–70s) say out loud that nobody speaks to them.** An ad that visibly includes a 60-something learner would stand out.
- **The AI voice teacher has a real but small, sceptical audience.** Wins: memory of past chats, practising MY words, a safe space for the shy. Losses: "too agreeable", "robotic", hidden pricing. Price must be visible before any quiz.
- **Anime teens are the loudest YouTube cohort but want comprehension and won't pay.** Don't aim ads at them.
- **ADHD learners depend on short, zero-set-up sessions.** "Just press go, no deck to build" is a real selling point versus Anki.
- **Anki earns "boring but it works"; Duolingo earns "fun but useless".** WordStick's pitch is the gap between them: proven recall that doesn't feel like homework.
