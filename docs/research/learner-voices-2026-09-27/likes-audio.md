# What Japanese learners LIKE about audio / commute / listening courses
Research for WordStick, 27 Sep 2026. Group: Pimsleur, Paul Noble, Michel Thomas, JapanesePod101 (Innovative Language), Glossika, Speechling, Nihongo con Teppei, Language Transfer, Assimil, Collins, "Learn in Your Car" / "Learn Japanese In Your Car".

**Method and honesty notes**
- **Audible reviews** (288 unique) were pulled raw from Audible's public catalog reviews endpoint (`api.audible.com/1.0/catalog/products/<ASIN>/reviews`) for about 30 Japanese audio titles on .com and .co.uk. These quotes are verbatim, taken straight from the raw text.
- **YouTube comments** were pulled raw with `yt-dlp --write-comments` from 12 review and learner videos (roughly 900 comments). These are also verbatim.
- **Web pages** (Tofugu, Goodreads, Apple, Trustpilot, HN, blogs) came through WebFetch, which returns a model-extracted summary. Quotes from these are *as extracted*. The Trustpilot ones in particular may be lightly trimmed, so treat them as lower confidence (marked ~).
- **Reddit was not fetched** because of its bot check, and I made no attempt to get around it. Amazon and StoryGraph blocked fetches too.
- **Caution on "Learn Japanese In Your Car"** (B0GNNVBWQ9, 2026): its reviews read as generic and possibly seeded. I use them only as low-weight support and never as the sole evidence for a theme.
- **Counts** are distinct sources (a product page, a video's comment section, a blog, a thread). They are not individual reviews. Where a theme leans on non-Japanese courses (for example Paul Noble German), that is flagged.

## (2) Why they pay
1. **A trip or deadline.** Japan travel is the most common purchase trigger across PN, Pimsleur, JPod and Collins reviews ("I have a summer job in Japan", B09LRFR8RH).
2. **Apps didn't get them speaking.** "DL doesn't get you speaking in its Japanese course, and I wanted more" (PN, 0008412537). Another: "I couldn't get myself to practice actually speaking and listening" until buying PN (same page).
3. **Structure they don't have to design.** "I've really been enjoying having such structure and progression" (Pimsleur, yulc7a6kiNs). Rocket/JPod comparison: pay for structure if you're not "confident you can structure your own learning" (IJV_stFpv2k).
4. **Ownership over rental.** "If I can't own it permanently then I won buy it" (-ASTlNdDABo). Another: "$200 ... lifetime access ... paying $400 is a steal" (yulc7a6kiNs).
5. **A free first lesson converts.** "I just completed the free lesson ... Pimsleur will be a great app for me" (hZLoqkT87-0).
6. **Cheap relative to the rival.** PN is bought as "budget Pimsleur" on a single Audible credit.

## (3) Why they stay / come back
1. **Buying the next volume is the retention loop.**
   - "I will be buying the next one." and "On my way to buy the second book" (0008412537).
   - "What's next, Paul? We would like a third book" (0008410631).
   - "I bought all other languages he has to offer" (UdPygXxcoEQ, 50 likes).
2. **The habit is welded to a daily slot.** Examples: the 25-min drive, the dog walk, the treadmill ("the only reason I could stay keep walking", N8DIBElL1Ow), and 15 min before bed.
3. **They repeat a lesson until the answer is automatic, and like that it lets them.**
   - "not moving on until I can readily answer each prompt" (B09LRFR8RH).
   - "I go over the same few chapters for a few days before I move on" (0008412537).
4. **Visible payoff in real life.** Coworker conversations, a phone reservation, Okinawa, "being able to talk to people on a call in japanese ... mad confidence boost" (drTGtJEvRCA).
5. **Always fresh content with a host they like.** Teppei: "new with every new episode".
6. **Fear of losing momentum when the course ends.** "worried I will lose the momentum when I finish the course" (yulc7a6kiNs). Another: "What do I do with my life now?" (0008410631).
7. **Why they leave instead:** price (N8DIBElL1Ow), school/busy life breaking the streak (hZLoqkT87-0), boredom ("I agree it is boring after a while", yulc7a6kiNs), and running out of course ("Why does it stop there?", B0036GROWW).

## (4) Small delightful details
- **Old words come back.** Old words resurface unannounced so you get "reminded when they came up" (PN), and learners call this the reason they never rewind.
- **Syllables built from the end.** Pimsleur breaks new words "into syllables, which is helpful" (Goodreads, Miri).
- **Mixing parts.** "given in little parts and then the bring back other parts and mix and match" (PN).
- **Slow breakdowns.** JPod's "slowed down breakdowns of words" and a playback that will "enunciate each syllable of each word" (B004BBE18G, B004FTSX4M).
- **Talking to yourself in public.** The walker who doesn't mind "people on the street thinking that I'm talking to myself" (UdPygXxcoEQ).
- **Speed as a dial.** Learners self-tune: 0.8x for more thinking time (B09LRHW258). Another: "it helps to speed up the course with every repeat" (PN).
- **Teppei's warmth.** Recurring jokes, the laugh, "calming"; a parent uses the beginner show "with my kids".
- **A named native partner.** Learners name PN's native speaker "Harumi" and credit "Paul and harumi".
- **Driving mode and CarPlay.** Pimsleur's driving mode / CarPlay / Android Auto gets mentioned as a reason to switch (-yeyKotgLHc; ~Trustpilot).

## (5) What WordStick could borrow (one line each, honest)
- **Own the slot.** Market "your 25-minute drive / dog walk", not "study time". The #1 like is dead time made useful.
- **Pace for thinking, not reading.** Make the answer gap generous by default and adjustable. It is Pimsleur's single most-repeated complaint on 3 separate pages, and PN wins on it.
- **Recycle old words.** Bring yesterday's words back unannounced mid-round. Users say that is what makes it "stick" without memorising, and it matches the next-morning check.
- **Make them speak.** Say the words out loud from minute one. "Had me speaking right from the start" is the confidence engine. WordStick already does this, so make it the headline.
- **Voice is make-or-break.** One warm, patient guide voice plus one clean native voice. Reviewers love or quit a course over the voice alone.
- **Never need the screen mid-round.** JPod walkers resent "ref' the PDF", so anything visual must be optional.
- **Let them repeat on purpose.** "Again until I can answer without thinking" is loved. WordStick's Again button fits, so lean into it.
- **Fixed, small daily dose.** A 15/30-minute framing ("split it morning and night") fits routines better than open-ended sessions.
- **Show real-life payoff.** Learners stay for moments like "held a conversation with a coworker". Prompt or celebrate those, not streaks.
- **Plan for "what next".** End-of-course churn and "What's next, Paul?" mean an always-deeper next set is retention, not a nice-to-have.
- **Price against the villain.** People paid happily for "budget Pimsleur" and resented $26/mo and per-credit splitting. Anchor WordStick below Pimsleur and avoid anything that feels like a split.
- **Honest limit.** Audio courses get dinged for thin vocab ("so little vocabulary ... useless during my trip"). WordStick's vocab-first depth is a real counter-position, so say it.
