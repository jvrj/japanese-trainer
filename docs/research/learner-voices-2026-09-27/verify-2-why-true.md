# WordStick learner-voice findings — independent "why it's true" evidence

Compiled 2026-09-27. Sources are peer-reviewed papers, regulators, company filings, and industry/government surveys. Learner comments are not counted as evidence here.
Grades: **Strong** means replicated research or primary data that tests the claim directly. **Moderate** means solid evidence for the mechanism, but it doesn't test the exact claim. **Weak** means indirect or secondary evidence. **None found** means nothing located.
Search budget ran out partway through the pass. Where a gap remains, it is marked "not verified".

## 1. Saying the word out loud from an English prompt builds speaking better than recognition or multiple choice
**Mechanism:** Retrieval practice (the testing effect): pulling a word out of memory strengthens it far more than re-studying it. There is also a transfer-appropriate match. Productive retrieval (English cue, produce the L2 word) builds productive knowledge. Receptive retrieval (see the L2 word, pick its meaning) builds mostly receptive knowledge. Output also forces learners to notice gaps in what they know (Swain).
**Grade: STRONG for vocabulary recall. MODERATE for "speaking ability".**
- Karpicke & Roediger (2008), *Science* 319:966. In this study, 40 students learned 40 Swahili–English pairs. One week later, learners who kept retrieving the pairs recalled about 80% of them, while learners who only re-studied recalled about 33%. http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Karpicke-Roediger-2008_Sci.pdf
- Nakata (2011), a critical review of flashcard software, found that receptive retrieval produces receptive gains and productive retrieval produces productive gains, so a good program needs productive mode. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254217121 . For a summary of the four retrieval types see https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2024.1496578/full
- Barcroft (2007) found that opportunities for word retrieval beat repeated study for both form recall and meaning recall. Productive retrieval helped both receptive and productive knowledge. https://www.academia.edu/125068632/Lexical_Input_Processing_and_Vocabulary_Learning
- Swain (1985, 1995), Output Hypothesis: output has noticing, hypothesis-testing and metalinguistic functions. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensible_output ; https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1095572.pdf
**Contradicting / limits:**
- (a) Productive recall of single words is not the same as conversational speaking. No study found shows a word-level spoken drill alone produces conversation.
- (b) Krashen's input camp argues that output is not necessary. The Output Hypothesis is influential but contested.
- (c) Nakata's own point is that productive-only practice under-trains recognition, which matters for listening (see Finding 3).

## 2. Years on apps like Duolingo and learners still can't speak
**Mechanism:** Apps are mostly receptive (tap, match, choose). Speaking tasks are short read-alouds or repeats, and there is no live interaction. App-only studies measure receptive skills well and speaking gains modestly.
**Grade: MODERATE. There is real counter-evidence.**
- Duolingo's own speaking whitepaper (Jiang et al., DRR-21-02, 2021) opens by admitting that Duolingo "has previously been shown to be highly effective at teaching receptive… skills. The question remains as to how well [it teaches] productive skills." It cites Lord (2015, 2016): Rosetta Stone-only learners "struggled in conversation". It cites Loewen et al. (2019): Duolingo Turkish learners "did not do as well on oral tasks". It also cites Loewen, Isbell & Sporn (2020): Babbel learners gained about 12 hours of study and moved from ACTFL 1.81 to 2.52 (Novice Mid, which means "isolated words and memorized phrases"), and only 59% improved in speaking. https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/duolingo-speaking-whitepaper.pdf
- The same whitepaper tested learners who finished Units 1–5 (Spanish n=156, French n=102) on the Versant test. On average, Spanish learners reached A2 and French learners reached low A2. 66% of Spanish and 53% of French learners were at A2 or above. The sample was heavily selected: only course finishers took part, only about 40% of eligible learners attempted the test, and suspicious scores were removed. A2 means "very short utterances… rarely able to understand enough to keep conversation going."
**Contradicting:**
- Kim, Payant, Skalicky & Namkung (2026), *Studies in Second Language Acquisition*, ran a 16-week quasi-experiment comparing Duolingo-only, classroom-only and classroom plus Duolingo. The Duolingo-only and classroom-only groups did not differ significantly on proficiency, productive vocabulary (picture description), grammar or communicative competence. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/comparing-the-effectiveness-of-duolingo-classroom-instruction-and-classroom-duolingo-instruction-conditions-on-beginnerlevel-french-language-development/68C0E7E296669798089C84CDC7F3BB9E
- The honest framing: beginners can't hold conversations after a few months by any method. The app weakness is real but relative. It is not that apps teach zero speaking.
- Duolingo efficacy hub: https://www.duolingo.com/efficacy/studies

## 3. The fear is of understanding the REPLY, not of asking the question
**Mechanism:** Listening anxiety is a measurable construct that is separate from general foreign-language anxiety, and it lowers comprehension. Its triggers are speech rate, no chance to replay, unfamiliar words, and too little processing time (Vogely 1998). The anxious learner then understands less, which makes the anxiety worse.
**Grade: MODERATE that listening fear is real and distinct. WEAK or NONE that it exceeds speaking fear.**
- Elkhafaifi (2005), *Modern Language Journal* 89(2):206–220, studied 233 learners. Listening anxiety and general FL anxiety are "separate but related" and both correlate negatively with achievement. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-4781.2005.00275.x ; https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ718481
- A TESL-EJ review (Chen et al., 2023) covers the sources of listening anxiety (Vogely 1998; MacIntyre 1995: "worry about misunderstanding or misinterpreting"). Learners with high listening anxiety scored lower (15.3 vs 17.9 out of 24). https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume27/ej106/ej106a9/
- Horwitz, Horwitz & Cope (1986), the FLCAS scale, includes items about not understanding (e.g. "I get upset when I don't understand what the teacher is correcting"). https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-4781.1986.tb05256.x ; items: https://dept.english.wisc.edu/rfyoung/333/FLCAS.pdf
- The Japan Tourism Agency surveys are real-world support: see Finding 10 (communication with staff).
**Contradicting:** The mainstream anxiety literature (Horwitz et al. and its descendants) generally finds *speaking* the most anxiety-provoking skill. No study was found that directly compares "fear of the reply" against "fear of asking" in travellers. So the finding is plausible and has a mechanism, but its "reply more than asking" ranking is **unverified**.

## 4. Speech grading frustrates learners, especially those with accents
**Mechanism:** Speech recognition models are trained mostly on native, "standard" speech. Error rates climb for under-represented accents and for learner (L2) speech. A model that can't recognise you marks correct attempts as wrong, which gives false negative feedback.
**Grade: STRONG for the bias mechanism. MODERATE for frustration.**
- Koenecke et al. (2020), *PNAS* 117(14), tested five commercial speech recognition systems (Amazon, Apple, Google, IBM, Microsoft). The word error rate was 0.35 for Black speakers against 0.19 for white speakers. The cause was the acoustic model, i.e. too little training data from those speakers. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1915768117 . Note: this concerns dialect, not L2 learners.
- **The most direct evidence comes from Duolingo's own whitepaper (2021).** About 28% of French test-takers (43 people) got *no score* because the Versant speech recogniser could not process them. Duolingo concluded that "the pronunciation of the participants in French was probably insufficiently clear for the Versant French speech recognition program." A machine grader rejected more than a quarter of real learners. (URL as in Finding 2.)
- A review in *Language Teaching* (Cambridge) on speech recognition in pronunciation learning notes that learners "may feel confused or discouraged if they are told that they pronounce wrongly every time", especially when accent habits are hard to fix at once. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/automatic-speech-recognition-and-pronunciation-learning/E399449253AFD9E7E3E9D65579023A9F
- For L2 speech recognition robustness remaining an open problem, see https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10738
**Contradicting:** Some studies find speech-recognition feedback *reduces* anxiety and improves pronunciation (Frontiers in Psychology 2023, EFL learners, mixed methods). Google Voice Typing recognised more than 90% of L2 speech in some studies. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10469312/ ; https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED625202.pdf . The frustration comes from *misrecognition*, not from speech recognition as such.

## 5. Learners want to see the written word alongside the audio
**Mechanism:** Orthographic facilitation. Seeing the spelling gives a second memory code and sharpens the sound representation. This holds for children and adults, and in L2.
**Grade: MODERATE to STRONG for the benefit. There is one caveat that matters for design.**
- Ehri & Rosenthal (2007), *Journal of Literacy Research*: exposure to spellings improved memory for pronunciations and meanings. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10862960701675341
- Ricketts, Bishop & Nation (2009), *QJEP*: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1080/17470210802696104
- L2 Chinese: both characters and pinyin boosted oral word recall compared with no orthography (Reading & Writing, 2019). https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11145-019-09955-6
**Contradicting / caveat:** Cerni, Bassetti & Masterson (2019), *Frontiers in Communication*: the spelling group learned more words, but their pronunciation became *distorted* by their native spelling rules (Italian double letters led to long consonants). https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00031/full . Implication for WordStick: romaji read with English letter-sound rules may distort pronunciation (e.g. vowel quality, long vowels). Kana is phonetically consistent. See also "friend or foe": https://pubs.aip.org/asa/jasa/article/145/4/EL265/849496

## 6. Subscription anger is mostly about forgotten free-trial charges with no warning
**Mechanism:** Consumer inattention combined with default auto-renewal. People forget. Friction in cancelling roughly doubles seller revenue. Apple does not send trial-ending reminders, while Google Play does.
**Grade: STRONG that inattention and auto-renewal cause unwanted charges. WEAK for "mostly forgotten trials". The complaint data point equally at hard cancellation.**
- Einav, Klopack & Mahoney, "Selling Subscriptions", *American Economic Review* 115(5), 2025, using payment-card data. Cancellations spike when a card is replaced and active renewal is forced. Cancellation frictions roughly double seller revenue. Inattention raises revenue by between 14% and more than 200%. https://www.nber.org/papers/w31547 ; https://web.stanford.edu/~leinav/pubs/AER2025.pdf
- FTC: complaints about negative-option and subscription practices ran at about 70 per day in 2024, up from 42 per day in 2021. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-click-cancel-rule-making-it-easier-consumers-end-recurring
- The FTC Click-to-Cancel rule was **vacated by the 8th Circuit on 8 July 2025** on procedural grounds, days before it took effect. The FTC sent a new Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking to OIRA on 30 January 2026. ROSCA enforcement continues. https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-click-to-cancel-rule-days-before-compliance-deadline ; https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/clicking-all-the-right-boxes-ftc-moves-to-revive-click-to-cancel-rule-following-eighth-circuit-vacatur ; https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/
- Australia: the CPRC report "Let me out" (August 2024) surveyed 2,000 people. 76% had difficulty cancelling an online subscription (including unsubscribing before a free trial ended), 45% spent more time than intended, and 10% gave up. https://cprc.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/CPRC_LetMeOut_SubsTraps_Report_FINAL.pdf ; https://cprc.org.au/release/trapped-paying-for-unwanted-subscriptions/
- The Competition and Consumer Amendment (Unfair Trading Practices) Bill 2026 **passed**. It bans subscription traps and unfair trading practices **from 1 July 2027**. Penalties are the greater of $50m, 3× the benefit, or 30% of turnover. https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2026/justanswer-fined--10m-as-subscription-traps-banned.html ; https://ministers.treasury.gov.au/ministers/andrew-leigh-2025/media-releases/unfair-trading-tricks-and-traps-be-banned
- Apple does not remind users when a trial ends (users must cancel at least 24 hours before). Google Play emails a reminder before a free trial or intro price ends. https://support.apple.com/en-us/118428 ; https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2020/04/building-user-trust-through-more.html
**Contradicting:** The largest measured pain in Australia (CPRC) is *difficulty cancelling*, not the surprise charge itself. The data support "trial and renewal traps plus hard cancellation", not "mostly forgotten trials".

## 7. Backlash against AI in language apps
**Mechanism:** Consumers prefer human-made content and want AI disclosed. "AI-first" messaging framed as cost-cutting reads as quality loss and job loss.
**Grade: MODERATE that the sentiment is real. The commercial evidence CONTRADICTS the idea that the backlash cost Duolingo much.**
- The backlash was real. After the April 2025 memo there were boycott campaigns and deleted streaks. Von Ahn admitted "I said some stuff about AI… I didn't give enough context", and said it dampened growth among younger users in North America. Duolingo stopped provocative social posts. https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/duolingo-ai-first-consumer-backlash-lessons/757133/ ; https://sherwood.news/markets/backlash-to-duolingos-ai-memo-will-hit-q2-numbers-morgan-stanley/
- The numbers (company filings): Q2 2025 DAU growth was +40% year on year, against +60% a year earlier and at the low end of expectations. But revenue rose 41% to $252.3M, paid subscribers reached 10.9M, FY guidance was raised past $1B, and the stock rose about 30%. DAU growth kept slowing through 2025. Duolingo attributes part of the slowdown to its monetization focus, and guided about 20% DAU growth for 2026. Q2 2026 came in at +23%. https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/3c8277ee-bc94-4f5d-9b77-0db3e46f88b8 ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26xpres.htm ; https://www.classcentral.com/report/duolingo-2025/
- Trust surveys: Pew found 76% of US adults want AI content labelled, and only 12% are confident they can spot it. https://www.searchenginejournal.com/pew-most-americans-want-ai-labels-few-trust-detection/556697/ . A Gartner 2026 figure (via a secondary source) says 50% of US consumers prefer brands that don't use generative AI in customer-facing content. This is secondary and weak. https://www.klaviyo.com/solutions/ai/consumer-trust-in-ai ; YouGov report: https://yougov.com/en-us/reports/54566-how-people-really-feel-about-ai-content-report
**Contradicting:** The backlash had little effect on revenue. It hit growth among young social-media users, not the paying base. No survey specific to 35+ language learners on AI voices was found.

## 8. Adults 35+ feel apps aren't built for them, and older adults learn vocab fine with the right pacing
**Mechanism:** Aging mainly weakens *associative* memory (binding a new form to a meaning) and speed. Self-pacing and extra retrieval let older learners compensate. Retrieval practice still works, with a smaller effect.
**Grade: MODERATE for "they learn, with pacing". The word "fine" overstates it, because older learners are slower. NONE FOUND for "apps not built for them" (not verified; the search budget ran out).**
- Murphy, Hargis & Castel (2023), PMC: under fixed timing, younger adults recalled 0.40 of the pairs and older adults 0.24. **With self-pacing, older adults' recall improved significantly.** They spent extra time on items they had missed, which is strategic compensation. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10038181/
- Nilsson et al. (2021), *Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience*: 160 adults aged 65–75 took an 11-week Italian course and did learn vocabulary. Baseline associative memory and hippocampal volume predicted how well. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8209301/
- Retrieval-practice benefits are "reduced but still existing" in older adults and stable over 7 days: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6990689/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30335442/
- Market signal: in Duolingo's 2021 speaking study, 51% of Spanish finishers were aged 35–74 (31% aged 35–54, 21% aged 55–74), and 37% were learning for travel. Older learners are a large share of *committed* app learners.
**Contradicting:** Selective retrieval can impair memory for *unpractised* items more in older adults (PMC11939593). There is a real associative-memory deficit, so claim "learns well at their own pace", not "just as fast".

## 9. Dead time (commute, driving, chores) is the learning slot
**Mechanism:** Listening-only audio places low cognitive load on a driver, and hands-free audio is legal for fully licensed drivers. But *interacting by voice* raises the load.
**Grade: STRONG that listening is low-distraction. For WordStick specifically this CONTRADICTS the idea that speaking answers while driving is as safe as a podcast.**
- Strayer et al. / AAA Foundation (2013), a 5-point cognitive distraction scale: radio was category 1 (minimal risk). Handheld and hands-free phone calls were category 2 (moderate). Voice-based email and speech-to-text were category 3 (extensive), the highest of the tasks tested. Audiobook listening was rated low, close to simple voice commands. https://aaafoundation.org/measuring-cognitive-distraction-automobile/ ; https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/06/130612092949.htm ; Phase II: https://aaafoundation.org/measuring-cognitive-distraction-automobile-ii-assessing-vehicle-voice-based-interactive-technologies/
- **Implication:** WordStick's drill (hear English, recall and *say* Japanese) is active retrieval plus speech output. That is closer to a conversation (category about 2) than to the radio (category 1). "Hands-free" does not mean risk-free. Marketing that says "learn while driving" carries road-safety and regulator risk.
- Law (NSW Road Rules 2014, reg 300): fully licensed drivers may use a phone's audio-playing function only if it needs **no touching**. A phone in a cradle does not make other use legal. Learner and P1 drivers may not use a phone at all. Fines are about $362–$481 plus 5 demerit points (10 in double-demerit periods). https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/nsw/consol_reg/rr2014104/s300.html ; https://www.mynrma.com.au/open-road/advice-and-how-to/road-safety/whats-the-law-mobile-phones-and-driving
- In-car audio (Infinite Dial Australia 2025): 84% of adults who were in a car in the past month use radio there, and 52% of Australians consume podcasts monthly. https://www.edisonresearch.com/the-infinite-dial-australia-2025/ ; https://cra.au/en/media-releases/infinite-dial-australia-2025-commercial-radio-reaches-15-million-weekly-with-total-radio-remaining-the-top-choice-for-in-car-audio . No in-car podcast percentage was verified.

## 10. A Japan trip is the motivator
**Grade: STRONG for the size of the market. NONE FOUND for the share of travellers who study the language before a trip.**
- JNTO: **42,683,600 visitors in 2025** (+15.8% on 2024's 36.87M), the first year above 40M. Korea 9.5M, China 9.1M, Taiwan 6.8M, USA 3.3M (+21.4%). Europe, the US and Australia combined rose about 22%. Spending was ¥9.5 trillion. The weak yen (about ¥150 to the dollar) is a driver. https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h02673/ ; https://www.travelvoice.jp/english/international-arrivals-in-japan-exceeded-42-million-in-2025-10-million-more-than-2019 . The Australian 2025 figure was not verified in this pass.
- Japan Tourism Agency: the 2018 airport survey (n=3,225) found the #1 difficulty was communicating with facility staff (26.1%), mostly in restaurants (28.5%). Visitors coped with translation apps (44.7%) and body language (42.6%). https://www.travelvoice.jp/english/the-biggest-difficulty-for-international-visitors-in-traveling-in-japan-is-communication-with-facility-staff . The 2024 survey (n=4,189) put language barriers with staff at 15.2%, third place. The top answer was "nothing" (51.1%) and the second was trash bins (21.9%). https://soranews24.com/2025/05/08/survey-asks-foreign-tourists-what-bothered-them-in-japan-more-than-half-gave-same-answer/
- Duolingo 2021: 37% of Spanish learners said "travel" was their primary reason (see Finding 2).
**Contradicting:** The language-barrier pain is *shrinking* (26% down to 15%), and translation apps are the default workaround. WordStick is competing with Google Translate, not only with other courses.

## 11. Pimsleur teaches few words per lesson
**Grade: STRONG (the company's own FAQ).**
- Pimsleur says each 30-lesson level teaches "between 300 and 500 new vocabulary items", with lessons of about 30 minutes and about 150 lessons for major languages. That works out to **about 10–17 new items per 30-minute lesson**, and about 1,500–2,500 items across 5 levels. Pimsleur frames this as deliberate: the course aims to teach how to "use and manipulate the language", not a word count. https://www.pimsleur.com/pimsleur-faq/ ; https://storylearning.com/blog/pimsleur-review
- Japanese-specific per-level counts were not verified. Note that "vocabulary items" can include phrases.
