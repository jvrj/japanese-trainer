# Learner voices: travellers and older adults (congruence check)

Date: 27 Sep 2026. Question: do our 12 claims (C1 to C12) still hold when we listen to **travellers and older adults** instead of Reddit and app-store reviewers?

## Scope

- **What we read:** 44 pages fetched in full. Rick Steves travel forum (22 threads), japan-guide.com forum (4 threads), Gransnet (6 threads, UK over-50s), Mumsnet (6 threads), Goodreads reviews of Pimsleur Japanese (4 pages), AARP (1 article), plus travel/learning blogs (offbeatjapan.com, antoine.fi, thetruejapan.com, travelbeginsat40.com) and 1 WaniKani "older learners" thread (that forum was covered before, so it's only used as a cross-check).
- **Where the voices come from:** about half the threads are about Japan. The rest are the same kind of buyer (older travellers from the US and UK) learning French, Italian, Spanish or German before a trip. Where a quote is not about Japanese, the table says so.

## New themes (not in our earlier research)

1. **The public library is the price to beat.** This was the most repeated money point. People borrow Pimsleur for free: "check the library" (pf), "I love that I can get it through the library" (pf, Goodreads), "Also check the library" (pf, RS). For this buyer the choice isn't between our subscription and a lifetime deal. It's between us and **free from the library**. So the ad has to say what the library copy can't do: it's fresh every day, it picks the right words for you, and there are no CDs.
2. **The real rival is Google Translate plus pointing at things.** The standard forum answer to "how much Japanese do I need?" is *none*: "pictures and point. It worked on my previous visit" (pf), "We used Google translate ... Worked great" (pf). Survival alone won't sell. What sells is the **feeling** of trying (next point).
3. **Being praised for trying is the reward.** "Several people outright praised my efforts" (pf), "Even broken Japanese is a thousand times more appreciated" (pf, offbeatjapan), "learn the formal (longer) versions ... incredible what a differenece it made" (pf; typo in original). The motive is respect and warmth, not being able to get by.
4. **The shock is the reply, not the question.** "they will answer you in French and you will be lost" (pf), "they quickly reply in English!" (pf, Gransnet), "can't understand spoken Spanish as it is so fast" (pf). They fear not understanding the answer. **Hearing the answer after they speak** is a direct fit for our drill.
5. **Some want to see the word as well as hear it.** "I personally prefer to see a word I'm trying to learn to say" (pf), "I'd like to see Pimsleur provide a printed version of the script" (pf). This is the most common complaint about audio-only courses. A tap to show the written card answers it.
6. **Silly sentences destroy trust.** "Potatoes don't wear hats" (pf) and "the fish had dinner with the horse" (called "totally mad" (pf), Gransnet). Older buyers take nonsense sentences as proof the app is childish. Our ads can promise *real words you'll use on the trip*.
7. **Older users are loyal to their habits.** Streaks of 1,162 days, 1,667 days, 2,420 days, 3 years and "almost seven years". A daily habit is something they're proud of, not a guilt trap. Pushing "no streak guilt" may not land with over-60s. Pushing "a daily habit you'll keep" may.
8. **Brain health is a reason to learn.** "Learning another language can help hold off Alzheimer's" (pf, Gransnet), "learning a new language is good for the aging brain" (pf, RS). This is a reason to buy that has nothing to do with the trip.
9. **Voice recognition that marks you makes people angry.** "The voice recognition was terrible and very frustrating to use" (pf, Gransnet, about Babbel). This backs our rule that the microphone never grades the learner.
10. **A family link is a reason to learn.** A Gransnet poster is learning Spanish to talk to her son's partner, and a 62-year-old wants to "finally talk to my 200+ relatives in Japan" (pf, WaniKani cross-check). This confirms the partner/heritage buyer from a new place.
11. **The trip date sets the timeline.** People start 1 to 10 months out: "Duolingo for 5 months before my trip" (pf), "about 10 months" (pf), "a little bit every day for a month ... while driving" (pf). A countdown to the trip date is a natural hook.
