## What this means for WordStick

- **P1 and D1 (11 and 10 sources):** the biggest pain is that learners can recognise a word but can't say it, and saying words out loud is the core of the drill. Lead the copy with that pain in their words ("I recognise it when I see it, but I can't say it").
- **P2 (10):** the review pile is the top reason people quit. The honest-count design with no pile is right; say plainly that there is never a backlog to face.
- **W1 and D3 (9 and 6):** long commutes are common, and they ask for "completely hands-free that I can just start". This is the WordStick buyer, in their exact words.
- **P10 (4):** in the car, long dialogues "melt my brain". Short known-word rounds beat podcasts here; show that as a contrast, don't just claim it.
- **P6:** "I can't make noise when I get home" is a real limit on out-loud practice. The commute (car, walking) is where speaking aloud is private, which makes it a selling point.
- **P3 and M1 (8 each):** trial-to-charge surprises are the loudest money anger. With a card-required trial, the charge date and price must be impossible to miss, and cancelling must take one tap, or reviews will say "trap".
- **M2 vs M3:** people pay when the value is plain (around $4–$5 a month, or about $50 a year, feels fair; Speak at $250 caused rage). $59.99 a year sits at the edge of the "steal" zone, so the yearly plan should be the one pushed first.
- **M5:** subscription fatigue is real. A lifetime option may convert fence-sitters later, but the Rosetta Stone backlash shows it must be honoured forever.
- **P7 (5):** speech recognition that mishears people generates hate. Keeping STT as a trigger only, never a grade, is validated. Never mark someone wrong on what the mic heard.
- **P8 and D1 (Arena):** people want an AI voice partner but say current ones "sound like reading out loud" and are "confidently wrong". The Arena's bar is sounding natural and staying at the learner's level, not being clever.
- **D5 and L8:** "zero embarrassment" is a praised benefit of AI practice. Use it for the Arena, and the drill can claim it too.
- **D6 and P9:** they want visible short-term progress, but hate nagging and streak guilt. The morning check is the proof moment; keep notifications rare.
- **P11 and L7:** they hate arbitrary or formal words and love "sentences you will actually use". Everyday word families and trip phrases are the right content bias.
- **D2 and W2 (7 and 6):** trips and Japanese partners' families are concrete motivators. A dated trip goal ("ready for Japan by 12 March") fits the date-plan gap.
- **D8 and D9:** offline audio and "speak only, no writing" requests exist, and so do drives with no phone reception. Offline rounds would be a real differentiator; check whether the PWA already caches them.
- **L1 and L5:** Pimsleur and podcasts win because they're hands-free, but they're passive or scripted. WordStick's position is "Pimsleur's out-loud habit, but it's *your* words and it proves you kept them next morning."
