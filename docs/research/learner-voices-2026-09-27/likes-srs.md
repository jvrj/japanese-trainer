# What learners LIKE about flashcard / SRS Japanese apps
Group: Anki (+Kaishi 1.5k / Core decks), WaniKani (+Tsurukame phone client), Bunpro, Renshuu, jpdb.io, Kitsun, Torii, Migaku (SRS side), Kanji Study.
Compiled 27 Sep 2026 for WordStick. 57 distinct sources (see section 6).

How I got the quotes: WaniKani and Bunpro forum threads were pulled as raw text through their public Discourse JSON. Google Play reviews were pulled raw with google-play-scraper (about 150 "most relevant" reviews per app; only 4–5 star reviews kept). HN comments came through the Algolia API, and YouTube comments through yt-dlp. For those, every quote was copied from the raw text. Quotes marked (pf) came from WebFetch page extraction: they match the extractor output, but I did not see the raw HTML. Reddit was not touched. Each quote is under 15 words.
"Count" = the number of distinct sources where the theme appears, with the per-app split in brackets. Play Store and App Store listings count as one source each, even when many reviews on them repeat the theme. So the counts are conservative.

## 2. Why they pay
1. **Lifetime ends the "is it still worth it" question.** "you don't have to make an investment decision again" (WK 73200). Buying lifetime also becomes a commitment device: "the longer-term investment makes me more motivated" (WK 59863). People wait for holiday sales (WK ~$199; Bunpro's December sale, Play review).
2. **Trying it free first earns trust, and they buy quickly afterwards.** "After a month of use I immediately paid for a lifetime subscription." (Bunpro Play). Being allowed to start a trial without a card is itself praised.
3. **The price feels small next to the content.** "Worth the $5/mo subscription." (Bunpro Play); "It feels almost illegal…" (bp 118557); "15usd is very cheap" (Kanji Study Play).
4. **One-time purchases beat subscriptions.** "No subscriptions. Permanent access to this information!" (Kanji Study Play).
5. **Paying to support the makers.** jpdb runs on Patreon (~$2k/month, HN 35568406). Renshuu Pro is bought "if you get a lot of value…consider supporting" (Play). The Kanji Study reviewer says "go buy the developer lunch".
6. **The free tier got them hooked.** WK is free up to level 3 ("I know that I will be paying for it once I pass the free levels", WK 38149). The Renshuu free tier "will absolutely get you where you want to be". People upgrade out of gratitude and for extras such as listening practice.

## 3. Why they stay / come back
1. **The app owns the schedule.** Reviews show up and all they do is show up too ("the website does the schedule for me"; "all you have to do is be consistent in your review", Anki Play).
2. **The streak is on their side.** Cumulative "days studied" and level count never go down, so coming back after a break feels like adding to progress, not starting over (WK 64082). There's vacation mode too.
3. **Proof it's working, out in the world.** Reading signs in Japan, kanji on the N2, "now I work in a Japanese office" (Anki, YT _MWtbI4IwfU).
4. **A comeback discovery.** "I came back and reset my account…found I remembered most of it!" (WK 38149). Lifetime owners reset and replay the levels (WK 74049).
5. **It fits dead time.** Commute, lunch break, morning ritual, "Better than doomscrolling".
6. **Belonging and rituals.** Level-60 threads, the cake, the durtle and Crabigator lore, and staff replying in person.
7. **The product keeps improving.** "It feels like the service is evolving progressively" (Bunpro 41188).

## 5. What WordStick could borrow (one line each, honest)
- **Cumulative counter that never drops** ("days studied" / words nailed) beats a breakable streak. This fits the owner's "honest metrics" call, and WK/Bunpro users say so directly.
- **A felt tick on a correct answer**, like WK's green box: in audio form, a short pleasant chime after the spoken answer. Cheap, and it's the dopamine users name.
- **Lifetime option plus a winter sale.** The top "why they pay" reason, and lifetime buyers call it a commitment device. Risk: it conflicts with the recurring-revenue model, so it needs its own analysis.
- **Trial with no card** is praised as trust-building, which cuts against the planned card-required trial. It's worth knowing that Bunpro's trial-without-card is itself a reason people review the app.
- **Real-world payoff prompts** such as "you know 212 of the words in [a common sign/menu]". This is the jpdb idea ("words that match what you read") in the form of a commuter moment.
- **Funny, memorable hooks** (WK mnemonics) could come in audio form: a silly one-line story read aloud on a word's first miss. Honest caveat: for some learners crude English mnemonics are a turn-off.
- **Level milestones plus a ritual** (the WK level-60 cake). A small ceremonial moment at 100/250/500 nailed words.
- **Personal replies from the maker.** "no AI or bot!!" and "personal reply from Koichi" are loved, and a 1–3 person team can do this.
- **Forgiving by design**: no punishment for a miss (Kanji Study's over-punitive mode is a named dislike). WordStick's morning check should feel like a check, not a penalty.
- **A clear gap to own.** Nobody in this group praises speaking out loud or hands-free use. Renshuu and Bunpro users ask for speaking or listening modes. Every "spare moment" habit quoted here (commute, lunch) is still eyes-on-phone, and WordStick's hands-free commute drill fits that gap.
- **Honest recall (typed answers are "more honest")**: the next-morning check is the WordStick version of Torii's "having to type it out is me being more honest". Market it with that same honesty framing.
