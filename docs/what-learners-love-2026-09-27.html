00The headline

They are asking for WordStick by description

On Reddit, beginners ask for a speaking drill that works like flashcard review: show me English words and then wait for me to speak the Japanese. Another: if there existed something similar but for speaking practice, I would be very interested. A trip-planner described a "Listen and Speak" mode and said they'd pay for it. And: repeat words just by speaking without eyes and hands. screen is no needed.

Meanwhile no flashcard or beginner app offers hands-free, out-loud learning, and Duolingo removed its hands-free audio lessons, which its users called by far the best feature. One said: If they come back, I will come back to Super.

The closest thing they love today is Pimsleur: out loud, on the commute. Its fans are our buyer. Its weak spots are exactly WordStick's strengths: it's expensive, it runs out, it has thin vocabulary, and it never checks what you kept.

02What each rival is loved for

Pimsleur, Paul Noble, JapanesePod101, Teppei

The closest match to our buyer (about 58 liking comments on Reddit, 288 Audible reviews). Out loud from lesson one, the drive and the dog walk, trip phrases, old words coming back. Paul Noble wins on giving time to think; Pimsleur's most repeated complaint is too little time. Against: $550 and $26 a month, Among the most boring resources I have ever used, stock phrases with no grammar, thin vocabulary (almost useless during my trip), and it runs out.

WaniKani

About 60 liking comments on Reddit. It does the planning, easier to stick with than Anki, mnemonics that stay, and what you learn survives long breaks. People pay to have the organising done: Wanikani is the single best use of my money. Lifetime pricing.

Renshuu

About 60 liking comments on Reddit, and it dominates Play Store praise. Easy to stick with daily, premade lists, free with no ads, a cute mascot, a tiny team that fixes bugs in a day. Commuters size quizzes to their train stops. Against: too busy, cluttered.

Duolingo

About 35 Reddit likes against 45 complaints. Loved for a painless start, the daily habit, kana, fun, short lessons and being safe to be wrong. Hated for hearts, popups (Nine different popups), ads, reshuffled courses, the $38 streak-restore ask, and long streaks with no conversation: I was really good at playing their game. I did not know squat.

Anki, Bunpro, jpdb, Kaishi decks

Anki: the best even if it's boring as hell, cheap, praised mostly by advanced learners; beginners can't work out the setup. Bunpro: a free month that converts (I tried the free trial for one month and I was sold, 66 upvotes), lifetime over ads. Nobody in this group praises speaking out loud or hands-free use, and Renshuu and Bunpro users ask for it.

LingoDeer, Busuu, Drops, Human Japanese, Satori, Mochi

Actually teaches kana, then kanji. Clear, warm explanations. Native human audio. Drops' 5-minute cap. Mochi's named review moment, "Golden Time". A streak that forgives one missed day. Lifetime prices and a free first section that convinced them. LingoDeer is far better for Japanese than Duolingo, and used on a ferry commute.

Praktika, Speak, Langua, ChatGPT Voice, italki

Cheaper than a tutor. No judgment. Correction by echo (repeated what I said, then showed me how to say it correctly). Human-sounding voices. It remembers me: one learner paid for 2 years because the tutor still knew facts about them months later. On Reddit, AI is valued as practice for people who don't have ready/free access to native japanese speakers. ChatGPT's top complaints: it cuts in when you pause, won't slow down, praises wrong Japanese, and every conversation starts fresh. Loudest anger: Praktika's hidden prices and hard cancelling.

04What they wish existed

"I really wish that Duolingo didn't force you to stick to the lesson order."

14,000 likes. The single most-liked wish we found.

  • A speaking drill that works like flashcards: English prompt, you say the Japanese, then you hear the answer. Asked for by name on Reddit. This is WordStick.
  • To actually hold a conversation. The most common goal everywhere.
  • To get by on a trip or with the in-laws: restaurants and train stations and hotels · talk with her family without feeling lost.
  • Something hands-free they can just start: completely hands-free that I can just start, from a driver doing 2.5 hours each way.
  • Private speaking practice before facing a person, for shy learners.
  • An AI partner that remembers them and uses their words: the app would create a talk around my words and phrases. If someone builds it, I'd probably buy it.
  • One thing that tells them what to do: I need something where I don't have to mix resources.
  • Cute and fun with real substance: a 70-upvote comment said they'd pay a membership for it.
  • Short-term goals they can see, offline use, and a one-time payment option.

05Who has the strongest reasons

  • Commuters by car, train, bus and ferry. Forums mention 1, 2 and 2.5 hours each way and a 3-hour bus ride. Pimsleur's Reddit fans are mostly this person.
  • Travellers with a date, often older: I am 68 years old and planning to visit Japan.
  • Partners, in-laws and heritage learners. The most emotional stakes: I can't connect with my grandparents (2,300 likes).
  • Busy parents and full-timers, for whom Japanese is the first thing dropped.
  • Learners aged 50 to 80, who say nobody talks to them. Posts at 63 (1,100 likes), 70, 72 and 80.
  • On-and-off learners over many years, and people with ADHD who need "just press go".
  • Not our buyer Anime teens and advanced readers. Loudest on YouTube and in Reddit's big threads, but they want comprehension, not speaking, and expect it free.

06Money

  • Fair $50 for a year… That's a steal · 4$/month isn't expensive · $15 per month… on month three an loving it! WordStick's $59.99 a year sits at the top edge of "a steal". $8.99 a month is inside the fair zone.
  • Shock Big one-off prices (Pimsleur $550), $26 a month, and I can't throw away $90/year without some careful consideration.
  • Why they pay:
    • A lifetime or one-time deal (the top reason on Reddit and in two other groups).
    • To skip the setup: you are paying for convenience… more likely to stick with it.
    • To commit themselves. Having paid is the motivation.
    • A trial that converts: a month and I was sold.
    • A trip deadline. Apps didn't get them speaking. Cheaper than a tutor. To support a small team they like.
  • Pay once is loved in four separate groups: Wow, a non-subscription business model in 2026 (66 likes) · Imagine if I used a subscription app for ten years.
  • Trust breakers: charges after a trial, renewals after cancelling, prices hidden behind a quiz, stacked upsell popups.

07What this means for WordStick

Already right, so say it louder

  • Give the drill a name people are already asking for: "speaking flashcards". English prompt, you say the Japanese, you hear the answer.
  • "It decides for you." Open it and it tells you what to do today. No decks, no setup.
  • Out loud, hands-free, in dead time is the top love. Ad angle: the audio lessons Duolingo took away, with your own words, and a morning check that proves you kept them.
  • No pile, no hearts, no popups, no scolding. The v9.63 landing line already says "no pile".
  • A count that only goes up beats a streak people call hollow.
  • The morning check is "honest recall". One user: typing it out is me being much more honest with my ability.
  • The mic never grades. Mishearing is a named hate.
  • Rounds of 10 or 30 already fit a train stop or a short drive. Say so.

Cheap wins to build

  • A soft chime after each right answer. Today the drill has no sound on a right answer.
  • A name for the morning check, the way Mochi named "Golden Time".
  • An adjustable, generous time to answer (Pimsleur's most repeated complaint).
  • Permission for a light day: "busy today? just do 10".
  • Small ceremonies at 100, 250 and 500 words owned.
  • "Did you use a word today?" Collect the stories for ads.
  • A "weakest words" round button.
  • A warm, light personality in the voice and copy, without a busy screen.

Ads

  • Open with "years of apps and you still can't say it", not "vocab".
  • Aim at commuters, trips with a date, partners and in-laws, busy parents, and people over 50. Show a 60-something in an ad.
  • "Pimsleur-style out loud, at app price, with far more words, and it checks what you kept."
  • Once the morning check proves it, "you'll still remember after a break".

Talk, the voice teacher

  • Pitch it as private rehearsal before real people, on your own terms, nobody's time wasted. Not a tutor replacement.
  • Never cut in when they pause.
  • Correct by echo: "you said X, people say Y".
  • Remember them and use their words. This is Talk's reason to exist.
  • A slow-down control and time to think.
  • Price shown up front, cancel in one tap. Price it against a tutor: less than one tutor lesson a month.

08Your five calls

The evidence pushes against current plans here. Nothing has been changed.

Card up front, or a free taste first?

Three groups praise trying without a card, and charges after a trial are the loudest money anger. But Reddit also shows a trial converting well: a month and I was sold.

My pick: keep the card for the $300 test, but make the charge date, the price and one-tap cancel impossible to miss.

A lifetime price?

Now the top reason people paid in three groups, including Reddit. Subscriptions attract distrust.

My pick: still park it until 100+ paying customers, then test $99 to $119 as a holiday offer, and honour it forever. The evidence for it got stronger with Reddit.

The drill voice

The drill uses the phone's own voice. Natural on your Pixel, possibly robotic on cheap phones, and reviews attack robot voices by name.

My pick: test on one cheap Android and one iPhone before the ads run.

Choosing your own order

The most-liked wish of all (14,000 likes), yet "it decides for me" is the strongest love on Reddit.

My pick: the app picks by default, and the category tiles already let people choose. Say "it picks for you, or pick any topic, any time".

How hard to lean on driving

Commuters are the clearest buyer, but Reddit, YouTube and the forums all carry warnings: hard in heavy traffic, and one safety warning. Train and bus riders praise the same thing without the risk.

My pick: say "the commute" (car, train, bus, walk), never show a phone in a driver's hand, and state plainly that it never needs a look or a tap.

09What this doesn't cover

  • Medium, Quora and Amazon block automated reading, so they were skipped. No bot checks were bypassed.
  • TikTok, Facebook groups and Discord were not searched. Older and travelling learners may be more active there.
  • Praise for AI tutors in Japanese specifically is thin. Most reviews are for European languages.
  • Counts are rough keyword or hand tallies, not exact.
  • A few quotes came through a page-reading tool and may be slightly trimmed. Check any quote on its source page before it goes into an ad.
