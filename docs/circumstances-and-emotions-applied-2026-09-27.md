# Installing Circumstances + Releasing Emotions, applied to WordStick

Sources: NHB Pro Theory Call, 23 Apr 2026, "Installing Circumstances" (3,471 lines);
NHB Theory Call, 20 Feb 2025, "Releasing Emotions" (5,630 lines).
Digested 27 Sep 2026 from the full transcripts. Owner is watching both calls.
Companion to docs/installing-compliance-applied-2026-09-26.md (v9.61 built).

Where the three calls sit together:
- Circumstances = the FRONT of every message: what changed, and what it does to them.
- Compliance = the LADDER after that: small yeses, proof before the ask, no "no".
- Emotions = the WORDS: every line is an act the learner is doing, written as they do it.

---

## Call 1: Installing Circumstances (in plain words)

A circumstance is "something that happened that causes something else to happen".
A fact about the world with a consequence, not a claim about us.

Why it matters: a claim ("this app works") is us arguing, and the reader's job
becomes resisting. A circumstance ("overnight your brain throws out most of what
you only read; it keeps what you pulled out of your own head") is a fact; the
reader reasons from it and lands on OUR conclusion on their own. Alen: "when you
do this, everything else you say goes up a lot in credibility."

The rules he gives:
1. Every sale is a change the buyer wants, and every change starts as a
   conversation. If your thing is not in the conversation, you are invisible.
2. Every argument is if-then. "If you can control the if, you control the then."
   The circumstance is the if.
3. It goes in the PRE-OPENING: the first line of an ad, page, email, screen.
4. Name the change, then name what it does to THEM (the by-product). Change
   reads as chaos; then show how you tame the chaos.
5. Change is the only thing people notice. Repeat the same old thing and they
   stop hearing you (rappers still rapping about thugging at 42).
6. Static market, changed person is fine: "your hormones changed", "you booked
   the flight", "you signed up for the class". Each new learner arrives inside
   their own change.
7. To remove rival options never attack. "X is great IF you want [the cost]."
   Positive with a catch. A door closes when it gets expensive, heavy, slow;
   it opens when it gets light and easy. If you are one of five, you have 20%.
8. Never say "you" in the negative. Externalise: the world changed, here is
   the cost that fell out of it.
9. "Let me think about it" means "let me see if I can get this cheaper".
   Install the catch BEFORE they go compare.
10. Payoff: people reach their own conclusion, so you can tell the plain truth
    and still sell.

## Call 2: Releasing Emotions (in plain words)

Feelings are not made by things. "They can't make feelings in the factory."
Every feeling is already inside a person; an ACT gives the mind permission to
let it out. Proof: you feel better on the first bite, before anything is
digested, so it is the act of eating, not the food. The release always comes at
the START of the act.

Most of the call is personal (self-worth, compulsions, "have to" language). The
marketing rule is at the end and it is simple: the reader's mind works the same
way. Copy is a chain of acts the reader imagines themselves doing, and imagining
the acts releases the feeling, and the feeling gives permission to buy. "The
close is the act of buying, which releases the emotions."

The rules he gives:
1. "Copy is nothing but a series of acts." Never describe the thing; show the
   act. "We don't care what Batman does in his spare time."
2. Active language. "You're getting", not "you'll get". Adding -ing turned
   passive into active and "always increased conversions" in his closes.
3. Stay inside the act, never dwell on the conclusion.
4. After a key line, translate: "and that's why…", "you'll notice…", "that's
   how you know". Not on every line; be subtle.
5. Slide the close from "you're going to get" to "you're getting" to "it's
   already yours, waiting inside".
6. Others can act too: the product, the teacher, the owner. Their acts release
   feeling in the reader without being noticed.
7. Empower = help them make decisions. Disempower = take decisions away.
   "Have to / need to / either-or" install low worth and no choice. Never use
   them on the learner.
8. "If you can't try it on, how are you going to buy it?" Permission grows by
   contact. Sit in the car first.
9. Fuse the goal to what they already love (the trip, the person) and the
   energy to keep going comes for free.
10. "Behaviour is the language of the subconscious." Same truth as Compliance.

## What WordStick already has right (keep)

- The drill is an act every few seconds (say the word). The release is at the
  moment of saying, not the tick afterwards. Do not move the reward to after
  the act (no confetti, points, streak fireworks).
- The morning check is a circumstance the learner creates for themselves and
  the act that releases the feeling. Nothing we say substitutes for it.
- v9.61 already put the act before the ask and removed every "no". Both calls
  agree with that order.
- Exits are decisions, not dead ends ("Keep going as I am", "Do it tonight").

## The gap

Almost every surface now either states what WordStick does or invites a small
action. Almost none opens with a fact that changed and what it does to the
learner, and much of the copy is future-passive ("come back and see", "you'll
get a check") rather than an act in progress.

### 1. Ads §J: circumstance line first, then the act, then the translate (HIGH, both calls)
Now: "Say ありがとう out loud…" (act only).
Draft J1 v3:
  "Most people who start Japanese lose their first ten words inside a week.
   Say this one out loud, right now: a-ri-ga-to-u. Put the phone down for a
   second. Still there? You just pulled a word out of your own head, and that's
   the only kind that stays. Tomorrow morning you'll be pulling it out again.
   That's how you know it's yours."
Shape: change, act, notice, that's why. Active -ing. Stays in the act.
Spender-audience variant built on the PERSON change (one ad per circumstance):
  "You've got a flight / a JLPT date / a class on Tuesday. Your brain has weeks,
   not years. Say this one now…"
Broad-audience variant, "what else changed and nobody realises":
  "Every app now has AI. None of them changed who does the remembering.
   WordStick makes you do it, once, out loud, before you see the answer."

### 2. Landing pre-opening: one circumstance above the demo (HIGH, Circumstances)
Now: "Try one word right now, nothing to sign up for" then the demo.
Draft, above it:
  "Overnight your brain throws out almost everything you only read. It keeps
   what you pulled out of your own head. Try one word and see which kind it
   becomes."
Then under the demo after they press play (Emotions, translate + contact):
  "You just did the act. Not read it, said it. That's the only kind of
   remembering that lasts, and you're going to be doing it every morning."

### 3. Landing: bring the rival paths back as "positive with a catch" (HIGH shape, MED owner call)
The knock-down list was cut on 27 Sep because it attacked. Alen: do not attack,
but do not leave five doors open either.
Draft, one short block under "Why it works":
  "Duolingo is great if you want a streak. It never asks you to say a word

   Anki is great if you want to build and grade your own cards. That's your

### 4. Round-1 end: the cause in front, in active language (HIGH, both)

### 5. Plans screen after the proof: by-product, catch on the starter plan, possessive slide (HIGH, both)
Now: "You just proved 8 words cold after a night's sleep, nothing on the

Draft:

   Try the whole thing for 7 days and watch the count climb."
The starter-plan door closes on pace (a catch), never on "free limits".
Add the carried rule (own the if): "A word that survived one night survives
the year with a two-minute check now and then. That's the whole plan."

### 6. Home banner: show what moved since yesterday (MED-HIGH, Circumstances)
Now: "8 words yours · 3 new today / Reviews never run out." (static; "reviews
never run out" is a feature line, the thing not the act).
Draft: "+2 since yesterday · 8 yours · 3 new today. Say them, keep them."
Needs a daily diff of the owned count (cheap: store yesterday's N).

### 7. Morning check screen: one bare line of circumstance (MED)
Now: starts cold, by design. Keep it bare. One line, no animation:
  "A night has passed. Say each one, then type it."

### 8. Talk: open every session with the change in the ledger (HIGH, fits the anchor)
Now: speaks first, greeting / first sound.
Draft opener (spoken, kana content): "Since yesterday you've got three new
ones and one that slipped. Let's start with the one that slipped." Then the
session tames the chaos the opener named. "Offer the first sound first" is
exactly Emotions: get them acting within seconds.
First Talk question, the fuse-to-what-they-love lever: "Who are you going to
say this to?"

### 9. Posts / notifications / email when built: every one carries a new change (HIGH)
Rule: never "come back and practise". Always what changed: "Two of yesterday's
words are about to fade. One minute keeps them." Posts open with a real
number once learners exist: "37 people proved a word cold this morning."
Owner-as-actor posts: a chain of acts, never the conclusion ("now he speaks
Japanese").

### 10. Copy audit rule for every screen (HIGH, both)
Two questions on every line, next to the Compliance test:
  a. Does it open with what changed and what it does to them? (Circumstances)
  b. Is it an act the learner is doing, written as they do it, or a
     description of a thing? (Emotions)
Strip "have to", "need to", either-or, and every future-passive "you'll get".

## Caveat on the facts
A circumstance only works if it is true and checkable. The overnight-forgetting
line needs one plain source on hand (the research citations were cut from the
landing on 27 Sep; keep one off-page) or, better, the app's own morning-check
numbers once learners exist ("people who said it from memory kept 8 in 10").

## Not from the calls (do not do on their account)
- No re-ordering of the funnel; Compliance already fixed the order.
- No gamification; Emotions argues rewards after the act move the release to
  the wrong place.
- The closing-doors block is the one item the owner cut last time; it is a
  different thing now (praise with a catch, not a knock-down), but it is his
