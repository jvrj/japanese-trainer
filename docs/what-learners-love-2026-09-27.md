# What Japanese learners love, hate and want: every other app, in their words (27 Sep 2026)

**What this is:** the owner asked "what do they like about the other apps? explore every single detail… YouTube comments, podcast comments, anywhere they pour out pains and desires." Eight researchers went out in parallel. This page is the merged answer. The full evidence (every quote with its link and count) sits in the eight source files listed at the bottom; this page points, it doesn't repeat them.

**How big:** about 21,000 learner voices.
- ~11,900 YouTube comments (54 videos)
- ~4,900 positive reviews of beginner apps (Play + App Store, 27 apps)
- ~2,900 Duolingo reviews
- ~290 Audible reviews of Japanese audio courses, plus ~900 more YouTube comments on them
- 55 forum threads, podcast review pages and Trustpilot pages
- 46 sources on AI tutors and human tutors
- Reddit (see the Reddit section; read through the owner's browser)

**How to read the numbers:** "sources" = separate pages or threads. "Likes" = the upvotes on a single comment, which shows how many people agreed. Keyword counts are rough.

**Honesty notes:** no bot checks were bypassed (Medium, Quora and Amazon blocked us; Reddit was read through a normal browser). A few quotes came through a page-reading tool and may be lightly trimmed; spot-check any quote before it goes into an ad. The YouTube crowd leans young and anime-driven, so our real buyer (busy adult, trip, partner) is quieter there than in real life.

## 2. What each rival is loved for (one line each; detail in the source files)

- **Duolingo:** fun like a game; the streak builds the habit; whole Japanese course free; gentle kana teaching; short lessons; safe to be wrong. Its **removed hands-free audio lessons** were "by far the best feature", and one user said "If they come back, I will come back to Super". Love turns sour over hearts, energy caps, reshuffled courses, and the $38 streak-restore ask.
- **Anki / WaniKani / Bunpro / Renshuu / jpdb:** progress you can see (levels, bars); the app owns the schedule; funny mnemonics; free and generous (Renshuu, Anki); lifetime pricing (WaniKani); a small team that answers personally. **Nobody in this group praises speaking out loud or hands-free use, and Renshuu and Bunpro users ask for it.**
- **Pimsleur / Paul Noble / JapanesePod101 / Teppei:** the drive and the dog walk; out loud from lesson one; old words come back; one voice they like. Paul Noble wins on giving **time to think**; Pimsleur's most repeated complaint is too little time. Loved as "budget Pimsleur" at one Audible credit; $550 and $26/month cause sticker shock. Criticised for thin vocab ("almost useless during my trip").
- **LingoDeer / Busuu / Drops / Human Japanese / Satori / Mochi:** actually teaches kana then kanji; clear warm explanations; native human audio; Drops' 5-minute cap; Mochi's named review moment, "Golden Time"; a streak that forgives one missed day; lifetime and one-time prices; a free first section that convinced them.
- **Praktika / Speak / Langua / ChatGPT Voice / italki:** cheaper than a tutor; no judgment; correction by echo ("repeated what I said, then showed me how to say it correctly"); human-sounding voices; **it remembers me** (Langua "remembers facts about me… even months down the road" kept one user paying for 2 years). ChatGPT's top complaints: it cuts in when you pause, won't slow down, praises wrong Japanese, "every conversation starts fresh". Loudest anger: Praktika's hidden prices and hard cancelling.

## 4. What they wish existed

1. **To actually hold a conversation** (the most common goal everywhere).
2. **To get by on a trip:** "restaurants and train stations and hotels" (53 likes).
3. **No forced lesson order:** "I really wish that Duolingo didn't force you to stick to the lesson order" (**14,000 likes, the single most-liked wish we found**).
4. **Something hands-free they can just start:** "completely hands-free that I can just start" (a 2.5-hour-each-way driver).
5. **An AI partner that remembers them and uses their words:** "the app would create a talk around my words and phrases"; "longterm memory… If someone makes a dedicated app… I'd probably buy it".
6. **One thing that tells them what to do:** "I need something where I don't have to mix resources".
7. **Short-term goals they can see:** "I also need short-term objectives to see my progress".
8. **Offline:** one driver loses signal in the mountains.
9. **Speak-only, writing optional** (this one is split: others want romaji off).

---

## 5. Who they are (who has the strongest reasons)

- **Commuters and drivers with long fixed dead time.** Forums: 1, 2, 2.5 hours each way, a 3-hour bus ride. They are the clearest buyer in the forums, a thin slice on YouTube.
- **Travellers with a date,** often older ("I am 68 years old and planning to visit Japan").
- **Partner, in-law and heritage learners, who have the most emotional stakes.** "I can't connect with my grandparents" (2,300 likes); "My wife is Japanese… how stupid I feel".
- **Older learners, 50 to 80, who say nobody talks to them.** Posts at ages 63 (1,100 likes), 70, 72 and 80.
- **On-and-off learners over many years,** and self-described ADHD learners who need "just press go".
- **Not our buyer:** anime teens. They are the loudest group on YouTube, but they want to understand shows, not speak, and they expect it free.

## 6. Money: what they'll pay, and what makes them angry

- **Fair zone:** "$50 for a year… That's a steal"; "4$/month isn't expensive"; "$15 per month… on month three an loving it!". **$59.99/yr sits at the top edge of "a steal"; $8.99/mo is inside the fair zone.**
- **Sticker shock:** big one-off prices (Pimsleur $550, "$150 for the BASE package?"), $26/month.
- **Why they pay:** a trip or deadline; apps didn't get them speaking; structure they don't have to design; paying once and owning it; a free taste that convinced them; removing a cap; cheaper than a tutor.
- **Pay once / lifetime is loved in three separate groups:** "Wow, a non-subscription business model in 2026" (66 likes); "lifetime… costs less than two annual".
- **Trust breakers:** charges after a trial, renewals after cancelling, prices hidden behind a quiz ("They asked like 10000 questions before being able to see the prices").

## 7. What this means for WordStick

### Already right, so say it louder
- **Out loud, hands-free, in dead time** is #1 and #2 on the love list, and **no app in the flashcard or beginner groups offers it.** Duolingo removed it, and its users miss it. Ad angle: "the audio lessons Duolingo took away, but with your words, and a morning check that proves you kept them".
- **No pile, no hearts, no scolding.** Say it plainly (v9.63's landing line does).
- **A count that only goes up** (words owned) beats a breakable streak, and learners say so themselves.
- **The morning check is "honest recall".** A Torii user: typing it out is "me being much more honest with my ability". That's the sales line for the check.
- **STT never grades.** Mishearing is a named hate, so keep it that way.
- **The app picks the words and the order** (they want "tell me what to do"), but see "no forced order" below.

### Cheap wins to build (each one is small)
1. **A soft chime after each right answer**, plus a spoken "nice" now and then. The ding is praised unprompted in three apps. Today the drill has no sound on a right answer.
2. **Name the morning check** the way Mochi named "Golden Time". People praise a named moment, not "spaced repetition".
3. **Adjustable time to answer** (and it should be generous by default). This is Pimsleur's most repeated complaint and Paul Noble's most praised strength. Check the drill's current gap first.
4. **Milestones at 100 / 250 / 500 words owned**: a small ceremony, like WaniKani's level-60 cake.
5. **"Did you use a word today?"**: ask for the real-life moment and keep the stories for ads ("this app made me look good").
6. **A "weakest words" round** as an explicit button.
7. **A short "why" on the first miss**, read aloud: a funny one-line hook, like WaniKani's mnemonics.

### Ads and targeting
- Lead with **"years of apps and you still can't say it"**, not "vocab".
- Aim at **drivers and commuters, trips with a date, partners and in-laws, and 50+ learners**. Show a 60-something in an ad; they say nobody speaks to them.
- New ad angle: **"the audio lessons they took away"** (Duolingo) and **"Pimsleur-style out loud, at app price, with far more words"**.

### Talk (the voice teacher): the bar in their words
- **Never cut in when they pause** (ChatGPT's #1 complaint).
- **Correct by echo:** "you said X, people say Y".
- **Remember them and use their words**, which is the thing reviewers say ChatGPT can't do. That's Talk's reason to exist.
- **A slow-down control and time to think.**
- **Price shown up front, cancel in one tap** (the loudest anger in the AI group).
- Price it **against a tutor, not against Duolingo**: "less than one tutor lesson a month".

### Four decisions for the owner (the evidence pushes against current plans)
1. **Card up front vs a free taste first.** Three groups praise "try it without a card" as trustworthy, and trial-charge anger is the loudest money complaint. The card-required trial was a locked call (23 Sep) for cold-traffic economics. **Recommendation:** keep the card for the $300 test, but make the charge date, the price and one-tap cancel impossible to miss on the plans screen and in the reminder email. Revisit if reviews say "trap".
2. **A lifetime price.** It is the #1 reason people paid in two groups. **Recommendation:** park it until 100+ payers, then test a lifetime price at under two years of yearly ($99–119) as a holiday-sale offer. It must be honoured forever (Rosetta Stone's broken "lifetime" is a named anger).
3. **The drill voice.** The drill uses the phone's own voice. On a Pixel it's natural; on cheaper phones it can sound robotic, and reviews attack robot voices by name. **Recommendation:** test on one cheap Android and one iPhone before the $300 run; if it's bad, recorded or cloud voices for the core words go on the roadmap.
4. **Order freedom.** The most-liked wish of all (14,000 likes) is not being forced through a fixed order, yet "tell me what to do" is also strong. **Recommendation:** the app picks by default, and the category tiles already let people choose. Say that in the copy: "pick any topic, any time".
