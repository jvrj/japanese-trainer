# WordStick Ads Playbook — distilled from Alen's Anatomy of Ads 1.0–3.0
*Built 6 Sep 2026 from the three recovered NHB+ calls (course lessons 04a, 17a, 39a in NHB-FF).
v2 same day: Part 2 (the operational test plan) added from the three guest calls — Ben & Loren
FB Ads Masterclass (b4), Loren on FB Ads (b5), Patrick Kenney on Google Ads (b7) — plus Alen's
written Anatomy notes (D:\ff-transcribe\transcripts\b*_ALEN-WRITTEN-NOTES.md).
This is the applied version: every principle translated to WordStick's $150 FB test, the ad
video cuts, and the landing page. Standing rule still binds: copy leads with the VOCAB outcome
in total-beginner words (no "kana/deck/SRS" unexplained).*

## 1. Who the ad is for — pick the state, not the person
- ~70% of the "learn Japanese" market is **unaware/curious** (likes anime/Japan, vaguely wants
  to learn "someday"), ~25% is **problem-aware** (tried apps, quit, feels stuck), ~5% is
  **solution-state** (actively shopping for an app RIGHT NOW). Everyone bids on the 5% —
  that's why "learn Japanese app" clicks are expensive and those buyers are the choosiest
  (they'll compare us to Duolingo et al feature-by-feature).
- **Our play: the problem-aware 25%.** People who TRIED (usually Duolingo) and quit. They're
  nearly problem-aware already, cheap to reach, and not jaded buyers. The ad's job is to pull
  them fully into the problem state, then walk them out into the solution state. People don't
  buy IN the problem state — they buy in the solution state; the ad must do BOTH moves.

## 2. The ad structure — Why → What → How (60/30/10)
- **Why (~60%)** = why + THEIR ACTUAL EXPERIENCE. Deep identifiers, no questions to build:
  - "Why you can quit a 300-day Duolingo streak and still not be able to say one sentence."
  - "Why the Japanese words you learn are gone by Friday."
  - Loren's frames: "…**without** flashcard marathons / textbooks / feeling judged" ·
    "**even if** you quit Duolingo twice."
  - It can never be their fault (identity market!): not because you're lazy, not because
    you're "bad at languages" — the apps drilled recognition, never RECALL. That sentence is
    the **aha-moment** — the moment they see why everything before didn't work is the sale.
- **What (~30%)** = the outcome, MOTIVATIONAL not educational. No mechanism lecture:
  "Imagine your commute quietly turning into 30 real Japanese words a week that stay."
  Sell the feeling of words sticking, not the method.
- **How (~10%)** = teaser only. "It works because you SAY the words out loud before you see
  them — here's the app that runs it hands-free." The landing page is the how; the click is
  the first step to the solution (= manufactured intent).

## 3. The 2.0 layer — one diagnosis line buys instant trust
Open (ad or landing page) with a TRUE description of their exact experience:
"You did the streak. You tapped the matching games on the bus. And when a Japanese person
said hello, nothing came out." Reader: *this person knows exactly what happens to me →
they must have solved it before.* Truth proves itself — pack verifiable proxy-truths and the
one new claim (recall-first works) rides in with them. State it; never ASK "struggling with
Japanese?" (asking signals we don't know).
Then: tell them WHAT to do, not HOW ("train recall out loud, in the dead minutes you already
have") — the app is the prescription, the "what to do next."

## 4. The 3.0 layer — criteria + sameness (the biggest lever for us)
- **Congruency: our buyer is SAMENESS-WITH-EXCEPTION.** "Become fluent / study Japanese
  every night" = high-difference = subconscious NO (that's the change that killed Eric's ad,
  CPA $300). The winning frame is KEEP: **"Keep your job, your commute, your evenings — the
  drive to work becomes the Japanese lesson."** Nothing new added to their life; a dead
  10 minutes upgraded. Prove the time cost (Alen: prove the sameness). This is WordStick's
  single strongest angle — say "keep", never "change your life."
- **Ad criteria to enforce in EVERY ad/cut** (and mirrored on the page — funnel congruency:
  short factual ad → short sectional page; our page, emails, onboarding must speak the same
  dialect as the ad):
  1. Not-your-fault reframe (identity market rule)
  2. Keep-frame (sameness with exception)
  3. Real numbers as proof (words/week, minutes/day — concrete, checkable)
  4. Removals — what they DON'T have to do: no typing, no flashcards to shuffle, no
     screen-staring, no schedule
  5. Hands-free/voice as the mechanism tease (the "new way" vs invalidated old ways)
  6. Free-start risk reversal (trial = low risk; price shown filters for buyers)
- **After the $150 test: extract, don't guess.** Winning ad(s) get dissected into their
  criteria; every subsequent ad keeps the skeleton, varies the skin. 10 random new ads =
  guessing; variations on an extracted structure = compounding.

## 5. Video-cut guidance (maps to the 4 takes)
- **15–30s FB ad cut:** Why-hook over the home-screen/drill footage (caption = the Duolingo
  diagnosis line) → 3–5s of the real drill with voice audible (the therapy/feel moment) →
  keep-frame line → outcome line → "Try it free" (front-door take). 60/30/10 by seconds:
  ~15s why, ~8s what, ~3s how/CTA.
- **30–60s landing cut:** same skeleton, one extra beat of the drill loop + the check-in tap
  (visible progress = Installing Progress principle: they must SEE change) + Sentences beat.
- Don't educate in the ad. Zero mechanism detail beyond the one tease. Motivation only;
  the page educates.
- Each desire level = its own future ad (desire stack: say-something-real → understood-when-
  traveling → connected-to-the-culture → proud-of-yourself). Ad #1 = say-something-real.

## 6. Landing page echoes
- Headline formula: "How to go from [quit-the-streak] to [30 words a week that stick]" —
  continuity with the ad's why (the missing 90% of the how lives here).
- Follow-ups/retargeting must RE-OPEN the problem (they forget it the moment they leave);
  first line of any retarget/email = back into the problem state, then re-bridge.
- Money follows trust; trust here = the diagnosis, not testimonials we don't have yet.

# PART 2 — How to actually RUN the $150 test (from b4/b5/b7, added 6 Sep)
*Part 1 = what the ad says. Part 2 = the machine it runs in. Sources: Loren (media buyer,
two calls) + Ben (copywriter) + Patrick Kenney (Google Ads).*

## 7. What $150 can and cannot prove — pick ONE question
- Loren's floor for verifying an offer is ~10× target CPA. $150 buys ONE question answered
  fuzzily. Ours: **"does this ad get quality clicks from cold strangers?"** Trial-starts are
  bonus signal, not statistics — with 1–2 conversions, CPA "is randomness."
- The isolation payoff: **good clicks + no trials = fix the landing page/offer, not the ad.**
  Bad clicks = fix the intro/hook, not the whole ad. Never conclude "ads don't work."
- A losing test picks "horses to bet on," not eternal truths — an ad that loses can win two
  months later. Don't over-index one $150 read into permanent criteria (refines §4's
  criteria-extraction: extract from WINNERS at real spend, not from this smoke test).

## 8. Campaign recipe (Loren's low-budget prescription, near-verbatim)
- ONE campaign, ABO, **ONE ad set**, **broad audience** (broad works on new accounts now and
  is the cheapest CPM; don't burn $150 building interest stacks — that's 2022-era buying).
- **Optimize for the real event** — trial start / Subscribe (use Facebook's STANDARD event
  names; FB has cross-advertiser data on what a "subscriber" looks like). NEVER the Traffic
  objective to save money: his controlled receipt — traffic test $148/475 clicks/0 conversions
  vs purchase-optimized twin $241/130 clicks/36 registrations at $6.70.
- One dynamic-creative ad: the video + 3–5 primary texts + a few headlines ("give Facebook
  something to work with" so a flop is interpretable). Skip headline split-testing — "hasn't
  moved the needle."
- **Exclude everyone warm** (page/IG engagers 365d, site visitors 180d, any list) so the read
  is pure cold. **$40–50/day over 3–4 days**, never one burst, never a 2-hour peek. Turn the
  ad set on after midnight. Ignore a slow day 1 if days 2–3 come good.
- Geo: the big six (US, CA, UK, IE, AU, NZ). Never worldwide.

## 9. Grading thresholds (the numbers to read the test with)
- **Link CTR ≥1% = passing.** CTR-all 2.5–3% minimum (5–10% good), and CTR-all should be
  ~2.5–3× link CTR. A ~1:1 ratio = bait clicks (image made them click without reading — bad).
  Video inflates CTR-all (play counts as a click) — allow for it.
- **Judge on CPC ranking**, not CPA, at this spend. CPM ~$30 is average; video CPM runs ~30%
  higher than image out of the gate — normal, ignore.
- Want ~1,500 impressions per creative variant (1,000 if broke) before judging — so $150
  reads 3–5 variants max, not 10.
- Kill rule if we ever scale a real budget: no conversion by ~3× target CPA in spend → cut.
- Clicks-to-landing-page-view ratio ≥85% (lower = page-speed problem).

## 10. The intro is the 80/20 (creative doctrine, both Loren calls)
- The only real estate that matters cold: **the 2–3 lines above "See more" + the image /
  first 3 seconds of video.** The intro must FIT before the fold — over-the-fold versions
  almost always lose. Draft against the mobile preview.
- **Fatigue fix = swap intro + image on the same proven body** — to a scroller that's a
  brand-new ad. (Client case: intros/images only → spend doubled AND ROAS doubled.) Never
  reshoot the whole video to iterate — re-cut the first 3 seconds + overlay + primary text.
- "Primary ingredients": 1–2 elements appear in EVERY variant (ours: the Duolingo-diagnosis
  + hands-free/speak-out-loud). Everything else rotates. Winning intro phrases get promoted
  into the landing page, emails, and the eventual VSL — intros ARE messaging research.
- Cold traffic needs **context before curiosity** — pure-curiosity openers die on FB; signal
  "this is for you" first (call out the experience, not a label).
- **Voice caution (refines §3's diagnosis opener):** second-person "You did the streak…" can
  read predatory / trip compliance on cold FB. A/B it against first-person ("I quit Duolingo
  twice…") and third-person ("Most people who quit a 300-day streak…") tellings of the SAME
  diagnosis.
- Soften absolutes: "often"/"can" instead of always/never — superlatives trigger "well, not
  me" and the message bounces.
- Ben's classified-ad test for headlines: would it pull answering only a phone number?
  Put the THING at the END of the headline ("…the free app") never the start.

## 11. New-account compliance discipline (matters more at $150 than anything)
- New/low-spend accounts get the hardest scrutiny, and rejections have MOMENTUM.
- No outcome+timeframe combos in claims ("30 words a week that stick" is outcome-rate —
  keep it; "fluent in 90 days" style = auto-reject bait). Avoid "and you can do it too."
- Never DELETE a rejected ad (the rejection becomes permanent). Appeal at most ONE.
  "Whitewash and abandon": edit a rejected ad into vanilla content, get it approved, turn it
  off, tag DNU — cleans the record.
- Free CPM reducers before launch: complete the Facebook page, post organically, answer DMs
  fast (FB tracks response time), reuse one post ID so social proof aggregates; reply to every
  positive comment with a question.

## 12. Free plumbing BEFORE the FB test (Kenney's $0 checklist)
- **Install GA4 + Google Tag Manager on wordstick.app NOW and link a dormant Google Ads
  account** — it feeds customer data from day one; a GA4 audience "landed from Facebook"
  auto-pushes into Google Ads as a ready retargeting audience for later. Audiences only
  accrue while the tag is live; ~30-day backfill max — data not collected is gone.
- Scroll-depth tracking on the landing page (GA4 enhanced measurement) — find where readers
  stop, reference that section in the next ad cut.
- Log any FB interest that ever converts (they port to Google custom segments later).
- After the test, if brand searches appear: a ~$1/day exact-match "wordstick" search campaign
  is nearly-free insurance on our own name. Google as a channel = later (its clock is 30-day
  increments and $100-at-a-time data buys; FB is the right first channel — Kenney's own
  sequencing for new offers).

## 13. Video-cut additions (fold into §5)
- ≤1:59.9 long (some placements refuse ≥2:00), 4:5 aspect, hook = first 3 seconds.
- POSER beat to add: a named-person example ("here's how Julius's commute became the lesson")
  — the proof beat Why-What-How doesn't force. (Until real users exist: the owner IS the case.)
- **Show the landing page IN the ad**: "when you tap this you'll see this page — watch for
  this section" (zoom on it). Viewers land pre-framed and hunt for that section.
- Alen's written 60/30/10 placement rule: the WHY lives in the VISUAL (the hook shot + overlay
  = the diagnosis), the WHAT in the copy, the HOW at the bottom/CTA.
- Question-opener bank (subconscious accepts what explains their experience):
  "If Duolingo worked, why can't you say one sentence?" — question → realize → agree.
- Invalidate the alternatives (obstacles ≠ objections): free apps drill recognition; that's
  WHY the streak didn't turn into speech. Names the enemy without naming brands beyond the
  one diagnosis line.
- Price in the ad = filtration for buyers (Alen). Test one variant with "$9/mo after trial"
  visible.
