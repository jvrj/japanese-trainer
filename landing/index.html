The trick is recall — and it's scientifically proven

Most apps show you a word and ask if you recognised it. Recognising feels like learning; it isn't. Isshin makes you do the hard, useful thing instead: it says the cue, goes quiet, and you have to pull the word out of your own memory and say it out loud. That act of retrieving a word — instead of re-reading it — is one of the best-proven effects in all of memory science. Isshin is built on it, end to end.

The proof: two of the most replicated findings about human memory — pulling an answer from memory beats re-reading it (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006), and spreading practice out beats cramming it (Cepeda et al., 2006). Every session in Isshin is those two findings, doing their job on your Japanese.

Questions

I know absolutely nothing — not one word, can't read a single character. Can I start?

Yes — that's exactly who this is for. Everything is spoken out loud, so you can learn words before you can read anything. The app teaches you the Japanese alphabet as you go, in small steps.

Can I really do this without looking at my phone?

Yes, for the core practice. It speaks, pauses, reveals the answer, and continues on its own. Glance at the screen whenever you want the spelling or a hint — it never requires it.

Is my voice recorded or stored anywhere?

No — nothing you say is ever recorded or kept. Speech is handled by your phone's own voice services, and answering by voice is optional anyway: tapping or typing works exactly the same. Details in our privacy policy.

What if I miss a few days — or a few weeks?

Nothing bad. There's no pile to catch up on and no red numbers waiting. A streak exists, if you switch it on. Nothing else is watching. You open the app, it picks up, you continue.

It runs in the browser — best on Android with Chrome today, fine on most modern phones. Voice answering is only a convenience, never a test — if the mic misses you, tap or type instead.

Yes — Language Transfer is genuinely free and genuinely good, especially for how the language fits together. Isshin's job is different: building your word stock and making it stick. Right now Isshin is free too — try both.
