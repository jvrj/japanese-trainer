### Verb Form Variants and Nuance

The **conjugation form slot is CLOSED: only three forms are used:**

1. **Affirmative past (-た)**: V-た方がいい
   - **Nuance:** Stronger, more assertive recommendation. Implies "this is the better action."
   - *Example:* 疲れたら、早く寝た方がいい。(If you're tired, you'd better go to bed early.)
   - Used when the speaker has conviction about the recommendation.

2. **Negative (-ない)**: V-ない方がいい
   - **Nuance:** Cautionary advice. "Don't do this."
   - *Example:* 言わない方がいいよ。(You shouldn't say that.)
   - Maintains present tense (ない) rather than past, even in an advice frame.
   - Note: Differs from affirmative, where past tense is preferred.

3. **Dictionary form (-る)**: V-る方がいい (softer alternative)
   - **Nuance:** Milder suggestion, like "it might be better to…"
   - *Example:* 自分でやる方がいい。(It will be better to do it by myself.)
   - Less common than past tense affirmative, but exists in some references.

### Comparison: Advice Strength vs. Other Patterns

- **ほうがいい**: Direct, assertive recommendation (suitable for advice to friends/family).
- **～たらいい / ～といい**: Softer proposal ("How about…?"), less forceful, more suitable for authority figures.
- **べき**: Much stronger than ほうがいい; implies obligation or strong moral stance.

## FRAME 2: COMPARISON — 「A (の)方が B より＋形容詞」

### Exact Structure

| Slot | Content | Nature | JLPT Level |
|------|---------|--------|-----------|
| A | Noun or noun phrase | OPEN (unbounded) | N5+ |
| (の)方 | Possessive marker (の) + 方 (hou, "side/way") | CLOSED (optional の; always 方) | N5 |
| が | Subject marker | FIXED | N5 |
| B | Noun or noun phrase (comparison point) | OPEN (unbounded) | N5+ |
| より | Comparison particle ("than") | FIXED | N5 |
| 形容詞 | Adjective (i- or na-adjective) | OPEN (unbounded) | N5+ |
| です | Copula (optional) | CLOSED (2 options: base or です) | N5 |

**Canonical forms:**
- **A の方が B より＋形容詞です**
  - 例: えいがより原作の方が面白いです。(The original is more interesting than the movie.)
- **Reversed word order (less common):** B より A の方が＋形容詞
  - 例: てんぷらより、おすしの方が好きです。(I prefer sushi to tempura.)
- **Shortened (when noun reference is clear):** A の方が＋形容詞です (yori implied)
  - 例: このかばんの方が安いです。(This bag is cheaper.)

| Source | Level | Notes |

**Consensus:** N5.

### Usage Rule: When to Use の

- **With nouns:** Always use の before 方
  - ご飯の方がおいしい (Rice is tastier.)
  - アイスクリームの方が好きです。(I prefer ice cream.)
- **With verbs:** Use 方 directly without の (rare in this comparison frame; more common with different structures)
  - Note: Japanese Professor and Japanese with Hikari note that "if you're comparing verbs, you don't say 'no hou ga', but rather just 'hou ga'."

### Relationship Between the Two Frames

**Are they the same 「方」(hou)?**

**YES.** Both frames use the kanji 方, which means "direction/side/way/method."

**Semantic connection:**
- In comparisons, 「の方が」 literally = "the side/way of [noun]"; it emphasizes which option is superior by framing it as "that side/direction."
- In advice, attaching 方 to a verb (V-方) expresses "the way to V" or "the direction to V"; the advice frame uses the same metaphor: "the better way to go."
- **Common root:** Selecting one option/direction over another.

**How sources treat this relationship:**

- **Tae Kim's Guide:** Groups both in "Using 「方」" sections (180–182), treating them as extensions of the same 方 noun with different attachments.
- **Maggie Sensei:** Has separate entries for "How to use 方 (hou)" and "How to use ほうがいい (hou ga ii)," but notes the common kanji.
- **Bunpro & JLPTsensei:** Treat them as separate grammar points (distinct JLPT entries), but both reference 方.

**Nuance difference:**
- Comparison: Both sides are neutral; you're stating a preference or fact.
  - 天ぷらより、おすしの方が好きです。(No moral stance; just a preference.)
- Advice: One side is better; implies a recommendation or caution.
  - 言わない方がいいよ。(Implies "don't" is the right choice.)
